# Research archive: Mencius, Laozi, and Mozi

Research date: 15 November 2026.

## Project decision

Proceed with Option 2: a browser-based ruler role-playing game in a fictional modern Chinese state, explicitly grounded in Mencian political ethics. Its central challenge is governing under practical constraints while meeting ethical obligations.

Option 1 is deferred: an imperial-setting game with one hidden standard of legitimate rule per playthrough, inspired by the Mencius, Daodejing, or Mozi. The principal unresolved issue is historical substantiation of decision-level judgments, not whether the hidden-judge mechanic could teach effectively.

This document preserves the exploratory research for Option 1. It is not a finished literature review, an approved scoring model, or a claim that the three traditions have cleanly separable policy positions.

## Evidence status and method

- The web research consulted scholarly encyclopedia discussions and Columbia University's translated primary-source selections.
- The chapter references below identify primary passages for further close reading. Most were not checked directly in a complete primary-text translation during this discussion.
- A scholarly overview supporting a general doctrine does not establish how its author would judge a particular fictional modern policy.
- Before scoring an event, record the primary passage, edition or translation and page number, scholarly interpretation, relevant circumstances, and reasoning behind the adaptation.
- Distinguish direct textual support from reasonable inference and speculative adaptation. Do not assign opposite scores merely to make the judges distinguishable.
- These traditions disagree internally and have complex textual histories. "Mencius," "Laozi," and "Mozi" are convenient judge names, not guarantees of single authorship or uncontested interpretation.

## 1. Mencius / Mengzi

### Relevant commitments

- Humane government includes securing the conditions for people's livelihoods. Material provision is an ethical responsibility, not a practical concern external to morality.
- Deprivation can push people into wrongdoing. Punishing people after failing to provide the conditions for stable livelihoods can amount to entrapping them.
- Ethical education and relationships matter alongside food, clothing, and security.
- Benevolence and righteousness constrain rule. Political effectiveness does not alone establish moral justification.
- Ritual and mourning can express genuine obligations, but ritual is not inflexible in emergencies.
- A ruler's status is morally conditional. The text discusses remonstrance, deposition, and the loss of the moral standing that ordinarily protects a ruler.

### Primary passages to investigate

| Reference | Relevance |
|---|---|
| 1A7 | Extending compassion; livelihoods; the problem of punishing people driven into wrongdoing by deprivation. |
| 3A3 | Livelihoods, taxation, and government arrangements. |
| 3A4 | Material provision and instruction in human relationships. |
| 3A2 | Mourning obligations in a ruler's circumstances. |
| 4A17 | Rescuing a drowning sister-in-law; judgment can override ordinary ritual restrictions. |
| 1B8 | The tyrant Zhou as a "mere fellow," rather than a ruler whose killing necessarily constitutes regicide. |
| 5B9 | Remonstrance and the different responsibilities of royal and non-royal ministers. |
| 1B10-11; 2B8-9 | The Yan intervention: authorization, popular reception, occupation, and wrongful conduct. Read together rather than extracting a general permission for conquest. |
| 7A45 | Differentiated relationships and the extension of concern. |

The Columbia selection directly consulted includes 1B6, 1B8, and 5B9. Other references need primary-text verification before use in game rules.

### Design guardrails

- Do not reduce Mencius to maximizing a welfare or popularity meter.
- Do not equate special family obligations with permission for corrupt appointments or unlimited favouritism.
- Do not assume all ritual expenditure is justified.
- Do not frame Mencian morality as always sacrificing effective government: competent provision is itself ethically significant.
- Do not present Mencius as interchangeable with all Confucian thought across Chinese history.

## 2. Laozi / Daodejing

### Relevant commitments

- Wuwei is not simply total inactivity. Interpretations include forms of nonassertive or noncoercive action, with important scholarly disagreement.
- The sage-ruler restrains self-serving desire, ambition, extraction, and excessive interference.
- Attempts to impose moral improvement can themselves be implicated in disorder.
- Ziran, often rendered naturalness or spontaneity, should not be reduced to a modern opposition between nature and all social institutions.
- The text criticizes heavy taxation, cruel punishment, and aggressive uses of military power.
- Its critique of ritual and conventional virtue differs from a straightforward calculation of economic waste.

### Primary passages to investigate

Chapter numbers refer to the conventional received-text arrangement; manuscript traditions differ.

| Chapter | Relevance |
|---|---|
| 17 | Restrained rulership and how people experience successful government. |
| 18-19 | Criticism of conventional moral categories and programmes; interpretation and textual variation require care. |
| 30-31 | Military domination, weapons, and attitudes toward victory. |
| 37 | Wuwei, desire, and transformation. |
| 38 | De, conventional virtues, and ritual; not a simple instruction to abolish every ceremony. |
| 57 | Restraint in governing and the effects of proliferating interventions. |
| 74-75 | Punishment, taxation, and the ruler's contribution to suffering. |

These are research leads supported by the consulted scholarly discussion, not a record of completed close reading of every chapter.

### Design guardrails

- Do not score "do nothing" as universally correct.
- Do not equate wuwei with abandoning famine victims or refusing all government action.
- Do not automatically translate criticism of moral cultivation into opposition to literacy, technical schooling, or every form of education.
- Do not assume the text offers a systematic doctrine of Heaven withdrawing a Mandate through portents.
- Specify a reading of the Daodejing rather than claiming to represent all Daoism.
- A hidden morality meter would be a game abstraction, not a mechanism described by the text.

## 3. Mozi / Mohist texts

### Relevant commitments

- Government and conduct should promote benefit and eliminate harm for the world. The relevant goods include material prosperity and social order, not simply treasury revenue.
- Inclusive care challenges harmful partiality and appeals to Heaven as an impartial moral standard.
- Worthy and capable officials should be promoted rather than selected merely for social origins or connections.
- Political order involves shared moral standards, exemplary superiors, instruction, and rewards and punishments.
- Authority is not unlimited: superiors can err and should be admonished; the sovereign is accountable to Heaven.
- Lavish funerals, prolonged mourning, and expensive elite musical entertainment are criticized for their harmful costs.
- Aggressive warfare is condemned, but defensive warfare is not categorically rejected.

### Primary passages and chapter groups to investigate

| Reference | Relevance |
|---|---|
| Chapters 8-10, "Promoting the Worthy" | Merit and official appointment. |
| Chapters 11-13, "Identifying Upward" | Moral coordination, hierarchy, admonition, and accountability above the sovereign. |
| Chapters 14-16, "Inclusive Care" | Concern beyond exclusive or harmful partiality. |
| Chapters 17-19, "Condemning Aggression" | Criticism of aggressive warfare; do not assume there are no distinctions among kinds of military action. |
| Chapters 20-21, "Moderation in Use" | Expenditure and public benefit. |
| Chapter 25, "Moderation in Burial" | Costs of lavish burial and prolonged mourning. |
| Chapters 26-28, "Heaven's Intent" | Heaven as a moral standard. |
| Chapter 32, "Condemning Music" | Costs of elite musical activity. |

English chapter titles vary by translation. The text has gaps, and chapter groupings do not imply that every original essay survives.

### Omens

Stanford's account of Mohist political theory quotes chapter 11 connecting destructive weather to failure to conform to Heaven. This gives a specific textual foundation for discussing weather as supernatural judgment in Mohism.

It does not establish that every omen type, a delayed feedback algorithm, or the same portent system across all three judges is historically warranted.

### Design guardrails

- Do not portray Mohism as secular economic efficiency with no religious commitments.
- Do not equate inclusive care with opposition to caring for one's parents.
- Do not infer support for every coercive policy merely because rewards and punishments are accepted.
- Do not assume maximizing immediate aggregate benefits licenses any act.
- Do not treat opposition to elite musical expenditure as an automatic verdict against every modern cultural programme.

## 4. Candidate cross-tradition categories

All three corpora address the following questions. That establishes coverage, not clean separation.

| Category | Mencian basis | Daodejing basis | Mohist basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Material welfare and burdens of government | 1A7; 3A3 | 57; 75 | Moderation in Use |
| Moral education and the creation of order | 3A4 | 18; 38; 57 | Identifying Upward |
| Ritual, mourning, and expenditure | 3A2; 4A17 | 38 | Moderation in Burial; Condemning Music |
| Warfare and justified force | 1B10-11; 2B8-9 | 30-31 | Condemning Aggression; defensive traditions |

### Candidate events and present confidence

1. **National ethical education programme:** promising contrast between relationship-centred cultivation, hierarchical coordination of moral standards, and criticism of imposed moral transformation. Needs separate assessment of each actual policy, not three labels attached to three buttons.
2. **State mourning after a parent's death:** promising disagreement about relational obligations, costs of prolonged observance, and reliance on external ritual. Distinguish sincere and proportionate mourning from extravagance and coercion.
3. **Ceremonial music funding:** potentially useful, but a Mencian defence of a particular institution cannot be assumed from generic Confucian support for ritual.
4. **Intervention in a neighbouring state:** useful for exploring justification, defence, occupation, and annexation. Avoid treating any tradition as simply pro-war or categorically pacifist.
5. **Tax-funded development with compulsory labour:** useful for material consequences and administrative burdens, but no automatic three-way disagreement has been established.
6. **Parental leave or family preference:** insufficiently grounded as an opposite-scoring event. Neither Mohist rejection of parental leave nor Mencian approval of nepotism follows from the general doctrines.

### Shared judgments are necessary

Relief from oppressive burdens, criticism of self-serving luxury, and concern about destructive aggression provide substantial common ground. Shared judgments are not a design failure. They prevent artificial polarization.

## 5. Outstanding design and historical problems for Option 1

- Distinct philosophies often support the same practical response, sometimes for different reasons.
- There is no verified universal weighting scheme for welfare, ritual, coercion, or ambition.
- Circumstances, means, intentions, and consequences cannot safely be reduced to fixed action tags such as "interventionist."
- A rigid judge would risk misrepresenting texts that value situational judgment.
- Omens as a common interface across all judges would need to be identified as fictional framing, not a shared historical doctrine.
- Using these three traditions teaches competing accounts of good rule. It does not by itself establish a chronological story of the Mandate's evolution.
- Material survival, ethical evaluation, and political legitimacy are related but not identical. A game's collapse threshold is an adaptation, not proof of a historical doctrine.

Before reviving Option 1, demonstrate multiple source-backed decision differences across an event sequence. Reject scenarios whose differentiation depends on caricature.

## 6. Sources consulted

### Scholarly overviews

12. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Chinese Metaphysics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-metaphysics/). Opening historical discussion supplied context for Zhou claims to Heaven's authorization.

### Earlier alternative candidates

Dong Zhongshu remains a possible source for a Heaven-human correspondence model, but overlaps significantly with Mencian concerns. Attribute received texts carefully rather than treating every chapter of the Chunqiu fanlu as unproblematically his.

Xunzi is useful as a challenge to automatic moral readings of natural disasters. His account should not be represented through a reliable supernatural omen-feedback system without acknowledging the contradiction.

## 7. Implications for the selected Option 2

- Start with Mencius explicitly, adding other Confucian sources only when named and justified.
- Build difficulty from limited resources, uncertain outcomes, competing obligations, and institutional limitations.
- Avoid a universal opposition between moral conduct and effective government.
- Judge whether policies responsibly serve people, not merely whether the ruler selects compassionate-sounding dialogue.
- Retain the planned post-run explanation, separating textual commitments from modern adaptations and game balancing.
- Option 2's detailed mechanics, event content, and implementation have not yet been agreed.
